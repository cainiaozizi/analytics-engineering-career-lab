# About Page Template

The About page is hardcoded in the React component.
When you're ready to update it, edit this file first — then copy the
content into `artifacts/analytics-career-lab/src/pages/about.tsx`.

---

## How to edit the About page

1. Fill out every section below with your real information
2. Open `artifacts/analytics-career-lab/src/pages/about.tsx`
3. Find each hardcoded string and replace it with your content
4. The page structure is fixed — change the text, not the layout

---

## Section 1 · Headline & Tagline

The first thing a visitor reads. One sentence that says who you are professionally.
Don't start with "I am a..." — lead with the work.

```
HEADLINE:  [Your name or professional identity]
           Example: "Analytics Engineer. Data Modeler. Occasional writer."

TAGLINE:   [1–2 sentences on your work philosophy or what you build]
           Example: "I build data infrastructure that helps product teams
                     make decisions with confidence."
```

---

## Section 2 · Bio

3–5 paragraphs. Written in first person.

```
PARAGRAPH 1 — Origin / how you got here:
[How did you get into analytics engineering? What drew you to the intersection
of data and software? 3–5 sentences. Be specific — "worked at X, noticed Y,
became interested in Z."]

PARAGRAPH 2 — What you actually do day-to-day:
[The real work: what does your day look like? What kinds of problems do you solve?
What do you care about in your craft? Avoid buzzwords like "passionate" and "driven."
Show, don't tell.]

PARAGRAPH 3 — Your perspective or approach:
[What's your engineering philosophy? What do you think about that most people
in your field don't, or don't say? This is optional but makes the page memorable.]

PARAGRAPH 4 — Outside work (optional):
[Brief. What do you do outside of data work? This humanizes the page without
making it a personal blog. 2–3 sentences max.]
```

## Section 3 · Core Competencies

Two columns of skills. These are NOT your resume — pick the things you'd
actually be comfortable being interviewed on.

```
COLUMN 1 HEADING:  [e.g. "Data & Analytics"]
COLUMN 1 ITEMS:
- [Skill 1]    — [one-line context, optional]
- [Skill 2]    — [one-line context, optional]
- [Skill 3]
- [Skill 4]
- [Skill 5]

COLUMN 2 HEADING:  [e.g. "Engineering"]
COLUMN 2 ITEMS:
- [Skill 1]
- [Skill 2]
- [Skill 3]
- [Skill 4]
- [Skill 5]
```

Examples of strong competency framing:
- "dbt — modeling, testing, macros, project architecture"
- "BigQuery — optimization, cost management, partitioning strategy"
- "Python — data pipelines, CLI tooling, pandas/polars"
- "SQL — window functions, performance tuning, dimensional modeling"

## Section 4 · Currently

A lightweight "what's happening now" section. Gives the page a sense of
momentum and gives returning visitors something new to read.
Update this every few months.

```
CURRENTLY WORKING ON:  [Project, role, or learning goal]
CURRENTLY LEARNING:    [Technology, concept, or skill]
CURRENTLY READING:     [Book or resource — optional]
OPEN TO:               [Consulting, full-time, writing, speaking — whatever applies]
```

## Tone guidelines

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Use specific examples | Use adjectives like "passionate" or "driven" |
| Name actual tools and systems | List every technology you've ever touched |
| Show your thinking, not just your titles | Write a cover letter |
| Keep paragraphs short (3–5 sentences) | Make the reader scroll past three paragraphs before learning anything |
| Write like you talk | Use corporate third-person |

## Checklist before updating the page

- [ ] Every link goes to a real, working URL
- [ ] Competencies are things you'd be comfortable interviewing on
- [ ] "Currently" section is actually current
- [ ] Bio has at least one specific, concrete detail (company name, project name, problem description)
- [ ] No placeholder text remains (no "lorem ipsum", no "your name here")
